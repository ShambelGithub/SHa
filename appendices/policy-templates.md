## 2. Startup-Friendly Evaluation Criteria

### Award Criteria Weighting

| Criterion | Weight | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **Quality and Innovation** | 40% | Solution fitness, innovation, technical merit |
| **Price and Value** | 30% | Total cost, value for money, pricing model |
| **Approach and Methodology** | 20% | Delivery plan, quality assurance, support |
| **Capacity and Capability** | 10% | Team, experience, financial standing |
| **Past Performance** (bonus) | +10% | Track record, references (not required for startups without previous government contracts) |

### Quality and Innovation (40 points)

**Excellent (35-40 points):**
- Solution exceeds requirements with innovative features
- Clear understanding of needs demonstrated
- Technical approach is sound and well-justified
- Significant benefits and added value identified

**Good (25-34 points):**
- Solution meets all requirements with some innovation
- Good understanding of needs
- Technical approach is appropriate
- Clear benefits identified

**Adequate (15-24 points):**
- Solution meets basic requirements
- Adequate understanding of needs
- Technical approach is acceptable
- Some benefits identified

**Poor (0-14 points):**
- Solution does not fully meet requirements
- Limited understanding of needs
- Technical approach has gaps
- Benefits unclear

### Price and Value (30 points)

**Excellent (25-30 points):**
- Highly competitive pricing
- Excellent value for money
- Pricing model aligns well with needs
- Clear, transparent cost breakdown

**Good (18-24 points):**
- Competitive pricing
- Good value for money
- Pricing model is appropriate
- Cost breakdown is clear

**Adequate (10-17 points):**
- Acceptable pricing
- Reasonable value
- Pricing model workable
- Cost breakdown provided

**Poor (0-9 points):**
- High pricing relative to value
- Poor value for money
- Pricing model problematic
- Cost breakdown unclear

### Approach and Methodology (20 points)

**Excellent (17-20 points):**
- Well-structured delivery plan with clear phases
- Robust quality assurance processes
- Comprehensive support offering
- Proactive risk management

**Good (12-16 points):**
- Clear delivery plan
- Appropriate quality assurance
- Good support offering
- Adequate risk management

**Adequate (7-11 points):**
- Basic delivery plan
- Basic quality assurance
- Acceptable support
- Some risk management

**Poor (0-6 points):**
- Unclear delivery plan
- Limited quality assurance
- Inadequate support
- Poor risk management

### Capacity and Capability (10 points)

**Excellent (9-10 points):**
- Strong, experienced team
- Highly relevant experience
- Solid financial standing
- Clear organizational capacity

**Good (6-8 points):**
- Competent team
- Relevant experience
- Adequate financial standing
- Sufficient capacity

**Adequate (3-5 points):**
- Team has necessary skills
- Some relevant experience
- Basic financial standing
- Minimum capacity

**Poor (0-2 points):**
- Team lacks key skills
- Limited relevant experience
- Weak financial standing
- Capacity concerns

### Past Performance (Bonus - up to 10 points)

**Note:** This is a bonus criterion. Startups without previous government contracts will not be penalized. All bidders start from the same base score, and this bonus rewards demonstrated track record.

**Excellent bonus (8-10 points):**
- Exceptional references from similar work
- Documented success in comparable projects
- Strong reputation in relevant field

**Good bonus (5-7 points):**
- Good references
- Success in related projects
- Positive reputation

**Adequate bonus (2-4 points):**
- Satisfactory references
- Some relevant success
- Acceptable reputation

**No bonus (0-1 points):**
- Limited or no references provided
- No demonstration of past performance

## 3. Fast-Track Procurement Process for Digital Services Under [Threshold Amount]

### Phase 1: Opportunity Publication (Week 1)

**Monday:**
- Opportunity published on digital procurement platform
- Email notification sent to registered suppliers
- Social media announcement

**Throughout Week:**
- Supplier Q&A window open
- Q&A responses published within 24 hours

### Phase 2: Bid Preparation (Weeks 2-3)

**Week 2:**
- Optional information session for potential bidders (especially supporting startups)
- Deadline for questions (end of Week 2)
- All Q&A responses published

**Week 3:**
- Bidders prepare submissions
- Support available from procurement team
- Submission deadline: Friday 5pm

### Phase 3: Evaluation (Week 4)

**Monday-Tuesday:**
- Initial review for compliance
- Clarification questions sent if needed (24-hour response time)

**Wednesday-Thursday:**
- Detailed evaluation against criteria
- Scores assigned by evaluation team

**Friday:**
- Evaluation completed
- Scores moderated
- Recommendation prepared

### Phase 4: Award Decision (Week 5)

**Monday:**
- Award decision made
- Successful bidder notified
- Standstill period begins (5 working days)

**During Standstill:**
- Unsuccessful bidders notified with feedback
- Questions addressed

**Friday:**
- Contract can be signed (if no challenges)

### Phase 5: Contract Mobilization (Week 6)

**Week 6:**
- Contract signed
- Kick-off meeting scheduled
- Advance payment processed (if applicable)
- Project commences

## 4. Payment Terms Best Practice for Startup Suppliers

### Standard Terms for Contracts Under [Threshold]

**Advance Payment:**
- 50% of first milestone payment paid within 5 working days of contract signature
- Provides working capital for resource mobilization
- No interest charged

**Milestone Payments:**
- Defined milestones with clear deliverables
- Payment within 15 working days of milestone completion
- Maximum of 10% retention until final acceptance

**Monthly Services:**
- Invoice submitted monthly
- Payment within 15 working days of receipt
- No advance payment deductions

**Final Payment:**
- Upon successful project completion and acceptance
- Retention released
- Payment within 15 working days

### Alternative Payment Models

**Option A: Monthly Subscription**
- First month paid in advance upon contract signing
- Subsequent months paid on 15-day terms
- Annual contracts can include quarterly advance payments

**Option B: Staged Development**
- Phase 1: 50% advance, 50% on completion
- Phase 2: 30% advance, 70% on completion
- Phase 3: Full payment on completion
- Each phase can commence upon completion of previous phase

**Option C: Usage-Based**
- Minimum monthly payment guaranteed
- Variable component paid monthly based on usage
- 15-day payment terms
- First month minimum paid in advance

## 5. Startup Eligibility Criteria

### Definition of Digital Startup for Procurement Purposes

A digital startup is defined as an organization that meets ALL of the following criteria:

1. **Age:** Registered and operating for less than 10 years

2. **Size:** Fewer than 50 employees

3. **Digital Focus:** Core business is development or provision of:
   - Software applications or platforms
   - Digital services
   - Technology-enabled solutions
   - Data analytics or AI services
   - Cybersecurity solutions
   - Cloud-based services
   - Other digital technologies

4. **Independence:** Not a subsidiary of a larger organization (exception: university spin-outs)

5. **Legal Status:** Properly registered business entity in [jurisdiction]

### Verification Requirements

**Simplified Documentation:**
- Certificate of incorporation (showing registration date)
- Latest accounts OR recent funding documentation
- Company profile describing digital focus
- Declaration of employee count
- Declaration of independence (or university spin-out status)

**No requirement for:**
- Extensive financial history
- Government contract experience
- Large financial guarantees
- Insurance coverage above reasonable minimums

## 6. Innovation Partnership Template

### Pre-Commercial Procurement Process

**Stage 1: Challenge Definition**

Contracting authority publishes:
- Problem or need statement
- Desired outcomes (not prescribed solutions)
- Budget range
- Timeline
- Evaluation criteria

**Stage 2: Expression of Interest**

Interested suppliers submit (maximum 3 pages):
- Understanding of challenge
- Proposed approach
- Team and capability
- Indicative budget and timeline

**Stage 3: Dialogue**

Selected suppliers (typically 3-5) invited to:
- Discussion sessions with contracting authority
- Refinement of challenge definition
- Exploration of potential solutions
- Resource and timeline discussions

**Stage 4: Solution Development**

Funding provided for development phase:
- Fixed-price feasibility contracts
- Typically 3-6 months
- Milestone-based deliverables
- Regular progress reviews

**Stage 5: Prototype and Testing**

Successful feasibility projects proceed to:
- Prototype development contracts
- User testing and refinement
- Technical validation
- Business case development

**Stage 6: Commercialization**

Proven solutions may proceed to:
- Direct award (within legal limits)
- Framework agreement placement
- Competitive procurement with performance data
- Recommendation to other authorities

**Throughout Process:**
- Intellectual property retained by supplier
- Non-dilutive funding
- Learning shared across participants
- Flexibility for pivots and iteration

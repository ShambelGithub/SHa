# Appendix A: Research Methodology Details

## Interview Protocols and Data Collection Instruments

### 1. Interview Protocol for Policymakers

**Introduction:**
Thank you for participating in this research on public procurement and digital startups. This interview will take approximately 45-60 minutes. Your responses will be confidential and used solely for research purposes.

**Section A: Background and Context**
1. What is your role in procurement policy development?
2. How long have you been involved in procurement policy?
3. What motivated the development of startup-friendly procurement policies in your jurisdiction?

**Section B: Policy Design**
4. What were the primary objectives of the procurement policy initiative?
5. What policy mechanisms were considered and ultimately adopted?
6. How were decisions made about specific design features?
7. What stakeholders were involved in policy development?
8. What challenges were encountered during the policy design phase?

**Section C: Implementation**
9. How was the policy implemented?
10. What resources were allocated for implementation?
11. What support mechanisms were established?
12. How were procurement officials prepared for the changes?
13. What challenges emerged during implementation?

**Section D: Outcomes and Evaluation**
14. How do you measure the success of the policy?
15. What outcomes have been observed?
16. What unexpected consequences have emerged?
17. How has the policy evolved since initial implementation?

**Section E: Lessons and Recommendations**
18. What would you do differently if starting again?
19. What advice would you give to other jurisdictions?
20. What future changes or improvements are planned?

### 2. Interview Protocol for Procurement Officials

**Introduction:**
This research examines how procurement officials experience and implement policies related to digital startup engagement. Your practical insights are invaluable.

**Section A: Background**
1. What is your role in the procurement process?
2. How long have you worked in public procurement?
3. What types of procurement do you typically handle?

**Section B: Perceptions of Startups**
4. How do you view startup suppliers compared to established companies?
5. What are the advantages of working with startups?
6. What concerns do you have about engaging startups?
7. Have your views changed over time?

**Section C: Practical Experience**
8. Can you describe a successful experience working with a startup supplier?
9. Can you describe a challenging or unsuccessful experience?
10. What factors contributed to success or failure in these cases?
11. How do you assess startup capability and reliability?

**Section D: Procedures and Support**
12. How have procurement procedures changed to accommodate startups?
13. What works well in the current approach?
14. What barriers remain?
15. What support do you receive for engaging startups?
16. What additional support would be helpful?

**Section E: Recommendations**
17. What changes would make it easier to work effectively with startups?
18. What advice would you give to startups interested in public procurement?
19. How can the balance between innovation and risk management be improved?

---

### 3. Interview Protocol for Digital Startup Founders

**Introduction:**
This research explores digital startup experiences with public procurement. Your perspective as a founder is crucial for understanding barriers and opportunities.

**Section A: Background**
1. Tell me about your startup (founding date, product/service, stage)?
2. What is your role in the company?
3. What motivated you to pursue public procurement opportunities?

**Section B: Procurement Experience**
4. How did you learn about procurement opportunities?
5. What opportunities did you pursue?
6. Walk me through your experience with the procurement process.
7. What barriers or challenges did you encounter?
8. What support or resources were helpful?

**Section C: Outcomes and Impact**
9. Were you successful in winning contracts? If so, which ones?
10. How did procurement engagement impact your business (revenue, credibility, growth)?
11. What did you learn through the process?
12. How did it compare to private sector sales?

**Section D: Resources and Capabilities**
13. What capabilities were needed to engage successfully?
14. What did you have to learn or develop?
15. What resources did you dedicate to procurement engagement?
16. Was it worth the investment?

**Section E: Recommendations**
17. Would you pursue public procurement opportunities again?
18. What advice would you give to other startups?
19. What changes to procurement policies or procedures would be most helpful?
20. How could the ecosystem better support startups interested in procurement?

---

### 4. Interview Protocol for Ecosystem Organizations

**Introduction:**
Ecosystem organizations play a vital role in supporting startups. This research examines how you engage with public procurement as a support mechanism.

**Section A: Background**
1. What is your organization's mission and focus?
2. What types of support do you provide to startups?
3. How many startups do you work with annually?

**Section B: Procurement Support**
4. Do you provide support for startups interested in public procurement?
5. What types of support do you offer?
6. What proportion of your startups are interested in public procurement?
7. What successes have you seen?

**Section C: Barriers and Challenges**
8. What are the main challenges startups face with public procurement?
9. What systemic barriers exist?
10. How do these compare to other startup challenges?

**Section D: Policy Engagement**
11. How do you engage with government on procurement policy?
12. What changes have you advocated for?
13. What has been the response from government?
14. What role do you see for ecosystem organizations in procurement?

**Section E: Recommendations**
15. What policy changes would most benefit the startup community?
16. How could procurement be better integrated into startup support programs?
17. What best practices have you observed?
18. What future developments do you anticipate?

## Document Analysis Framework

### Categories for Policy Document Analysis

**1. Policy Objectives and Rationale**
- Stated goals and objectives
- Justification and problem definition
- Stakeholder interests addressed
- Success criteria and metrics

**2. Procedural Requirements**
- Eligibility criteria
- Application and bidding processes
- Documentation requirements
- Timeline and stages
- Evaluation criteria

**3. Simplification Measures**
- Streamlined procedures
- Reduced documentation
- Faster timelines
- Digital tools and platforms

**4. Financial and Eligibility Criteria**
- Contract size ranges
- Financial requirements (guarantees, insurance)
- Payment terms
- Company age and size criteria

**5. Support Mechanisms**
- Training and guidance
- Dedicated support units
- Mentoring programs
- Technical assistance

**6. Monitoring and Evaluation**
- Data collection approaches
- Key performance indicators
- Evaluation methods
- Reporting requirements

## Validity and Reliability Measures

### Construct Validity

**Multiple Data Sources:**
- Policy documents
- Interview transcripts
- Procurement data
- Startup databases
- Academic literature

**Clear Operational Definitions:**
- Digital startup: Technology-based venture <10 years old
- Procurement engagement: Bidding on or winning public contracts
- Sustainability: Continued operation and growth indicators

**Case Study Protocol:**
- Standardized questions
- Data collection procedures
- Analysis approach

### Internal Validity

**Pattern Matching:**
- Compare empirical patterns to predicted patterns
- Look for consistency across multiple cases

**Explanation Building:**
- Iteratively develop explanations
- Test against rival explanations

**Time-Series Analysis:**
- Track changes over time
- Establish sequence of events

**Rival Explanations Considered:**
- Selection bias (more capable startups engage)
- General economic conditions
- Other policy interventions
- Sector-specific factors

### External Validity

**Multiple Case Selection:**
- Different jurisdictions
- Varied approaches
- Different contexts

**Theoretical Replication:**
- Predict similar results for similar conditions
- Predict different results for different conditions

**Clear Scope Conditions:**
- Digital startups specifically
- Central government procurement
- Developed economy contexts

### Reliability

**Detailed Documentation:**
- Research protocol documented
- Procedures clearly specified
- Decisions and rationale recorded

**Interview Protocols:**
- Standardized questions
- Consistent approach across interviewees
- Recording and transcription procedures

**Data Management:**
- Systematic filing and organization
- Version control
- Backup procedures

**Audit Trail:**
- Raw data preserved
- Analysis steps documented
- Interpretations justified with evidence

## Ethical Considerations

### Informed Consent

**Participant Information:**
- Research purpose and methods
- Expected time commitment
- How data will be used
- Confidentiality protections
- Right to withdraw

**Consent Documentation:**
- Written consent forms
- Verbal consent recorded (for phone interviews)
- Consent for recording (where applicable)

### Confidentiality Protections

**Data Handling:**
- Secure storage of interview recordings and transcripts
- Anonymization of sensitive information
- Aggregation where necessary
- Restricted access to raw data

**Reporting:**
- Quotes anonymized or attributed with permission
- Sensitive information excluded
- Company names used only with permission
- Context provided without identifying details

### Research Integrity

**Accurate Representation:**
- Quotes used in context
- Diverse viewpoints included
- Limitations acknowledged
- Negative findings reported

**Disclosure:**
- Funding sources disclosed
- Conflicts of interest identified
- Limitations clearly stated
- Researcher positionality acknowledged
